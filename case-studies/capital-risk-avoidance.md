## Context & Stakes

Following the programme recovery, a major technology supplier proposed a mainframe-to-cloud migration, positioned as cost reduction. The investment would have been the largest regional IT commitment in the group's history.

## The Real Problem (Not the Stated One)

<div class="diagnosis phase-control">
  <div class="card"><h3>The Proposal</h3><p>A supplier-promoted mainframe migration, framed as modernization and cost reduction. Required significant, multi-year capital commitment.</p></div>
  <div class="card findings"><h3>My Findings</h3><p>Cost allocation models were non-standardized. After benchmarking, the mainframe was among the most cost-efficient platforms in the group. Four mission-critical tools had been omitted from the migration analysis.</p></div>
</div>

## Key Interventions

<ul class="interventions">
  <li><strong>Independent economic analysis:</strong> Benchmarked cost structures across group entities, exposing flawed assumptions.</li>
  <li><strong>Escalation to corporate finance:</strong> Elevated the decision from IT to finance, reframing as capital allocation.</li>
  <li><strong>Knowledge preservation:</strong> Rebuilt critical documentation before senior engineer retirements.</li>
</ul>

## Measured Outcomes

Averted a high-risk, zero-value migration. Preserved a cost-efficient, stable platform. Strengthened executive confidence in capital allocation discipline.

## Why This Case Is Reusable

Any organization receiving vendor-promoted capital expenditure proposals should ask: "Has anyone independently validated the economics?" The pattern is pervasive across industries.
